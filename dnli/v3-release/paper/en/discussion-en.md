## 5 Discussion

### 5.1 Main findings

Even though everyday defeasible reasoning in δ-SNLI provides no numerical ground-truth probability, models exhibit structured regularities across the Multi-Candidate Verbalization (MCV) family. All 15 formal configurations reject H1 under single-sentence direction-controlled updates (+16.1 to +28.9 points); 13/15 reject H2 (+2.0 to +8.9 points, accompanied by elevated reversal rates), and all 15 reject H4a (+0.065 to +0.391 endpoint-rate proportion). Reporting effects are not established for gpt-oss-120b or sf:qwen3.5-4b. Multi-Candidate Weighted (MCW, M_W) directional costs (H4b) vary from −13.7 to +0.5 points, while exploratory candidate readings across 14 of the 15 formal configurations follow the point-estimate ordering Top-1 > MCW (M_W) > MCU (M_U). Eight un-harnessed formal API configurations and the 20-configuration dev ladder demonstrate that small MCW directional costs and stated narrowing/reported widening occur without a CLI harness. At the item level, 44.9%–77.3% of Stated Direct update reversals involve shifts of |Δ| ≤ 10 points and 53.8%–84.2% preserve pair ordering (s_i ≥ 0), while open-weight configurations under MCW retain substantially higher mode-preserving tail counterfactual rates (TailCF% 19.4%–36.7%, reaching 28.6%–36.7% in sf:glm-4-9b, Qwen3-8B, sf:qwen3-8b, aistudio:gemma-4-26b-a4b-it, and gpt-oss-120b, vs. 5.8%–15.8% in the four narrow-spread CLI-harnessed frontier configurations) alongside coherent physical, temporal, and hearsay counterfactuals. The exploratory two-model OpenAI 1+1+4 ablation further distinguishes Direct (D0), Direct-CoT (D_CoT), MCU (V2), and MCW (V0), showing that single-estimate Direct-CoT improves ordinal polarity discrimination while leaving first-numeric-token entropy below 1 bit and proposition entropy gains small, whereas omitting weights in MCU (V2) recovers directional movement on smaller models while halving candidate spread and proposition entropy gain relative to MCW (V0). These findings distinguish endpoint reduction from evidence sensitivity.

### 5.2 Directional sensitivity: the models move, but not every time

H1 shows that these models do not ignore new information: strengtheners raised the probability and weakeners lowered it, and the posterior difference D between strengtheners and weakeners was 35 to 57 points across the original and CLI extension configurations. Item by item, however, 16% to 29% of update sentences left the reported probability unchanged for the local models, and 7% to 15% did so for codex and the four extension configurations. One possibility is that, under direct elicitation, the models tend to report a few common round numbers; when the prior and the posterior fall on the same round number, no movement can be measured. The between-call variability reported in the Results section (for the local models, two reports for the same prompt differed by 8 to 10 points on average) is also a reminder that a single reported value is coarse, and that conclusions must rest on averages over many pairs.

We do not claim that these movements are "too large" or "too small." δ-NLI annotates only direction and provides no reference for an appropriate magnitude; answering the magnitude question would require people to report probabilities under the same paradigm (see 5.9).

### 5.3 Reporting wording: smaller movement, more reversals

Replacing "Here is something else you learned" with "Someone said that" reduced the directional movement in all three original configurations (by 2.0 to 5.4 points) and in three of the four extension configurations (by 3.1 to 4.1 points); for gpt-oss-120b the difference was 0.9 points and its direction was not established. If the report merely led the model to discount the source somewhat, one simple expectation would be a proportional shrinkage of movement in the original direction; but we also observed a rise in the reversal rate (e.g., from 0.16 to 0.22 for olmo2). The reversal rate was higher under the report in all seven configurations, including gpt-oss-120b. Following the pre-specified rule, we therefore state this result as "reporting shifts the direction of movement." Discounting as a mechanism was neither tested nor ruled out in this study. Within these original and CLI extension configurations, only for codex does the point estimate reach the 5-point smallest effect size of interest (SESOI), and for no configuration does the adjusted bound exceed it. In the eight un-harnessed REST API formal configurations (Table 9), five reach the 5-point SESOI in point estimate (+5.62 to +8.94), and aistudio:gemini-3.5-flash-lite has its Holm-adjusted lower bound (+5.95) exceed 5 points. Across those seven configurations, then, the models register that a statement is a report, but the resulting change in how far they move is small relative to the movement itself: the point estimates correspond to a reduction of roughly 5% to 25% of the stated movement.

On the common 120 Block-A pairs, un-harnessed sf:qwen3-8b and local Qwen3 (thinking off) agree in the direction of all four point estimates: H1 +18.72 vs. +18.90 points, H4a +0.265 vs. +0.254 proportion (a +1.0 percentage-point difference using unrounded values), H2 +3.78 vs. +1.67 points, and H4b −9.03 vs. −6.70 points. H1, H4a and H4b reject in both. API H2 rejects with 95% CI [1.48, 6.13]; local H2 rejects on the full 240-pair split (+1.97, [0.39, 3.61], p = 0.007), but does not reject on Block A alone ([−0.61, 3.94], p = 0.0748). Thus the common-subset comparison supports directional consistency without establishing equivalence between calling methods.

One explanation is pragmatic: in everyday conversation, going out of one's way to say "someone said p" sometimes implies that the speaker does not fully believe p; if the models have learned this pragmatic implication, they may treat the report as expressing some reservation about p, or even as a cue against it. Another explanation is that "Someone said that" turns the update into the event of "someone having said something," so that the model instead reasons about the speech situation rather than about the content of the update. Linguistic work on the extent to which "X said that p" licenses p (Ross & Pavlick, 2019; Jiang & de Marneffe, 2019) is compatible with both explanations, but the dependent variable in that work is an entailment judgment, not the probability movement for the same hypothesis.

Broken down by update type, the configurations show different patterns: for olmo2 the difference lies almost entirely in strengtheners, whereas for codex, claude-sonnet-5.5 and gemini-3.8-flash (low) it lies mainly in weakeners. This is a post hoc description, and no per-type test was conducted; it suggests that reporting may act in different ways on different models, which merits testing with more reporting wordings (e.g., named sources, sources of differing credibility).

### 5.4 Multi-estimate elicitation: de-extremizing and desensitizing

Multi-estimate elicitation achieved one of its expected effects: endpoint values fell sharply (for the local models, the weighted endpoint rate dropped from about 30%–42% to about 5%). This matters for research on probability updating: if the prior is already reported as 100%, a strengthener has no room to move it upward.

On the local models, however, the same elicitation also clearly reduced the directional movement (by about 43% and 68%). Item by item, the change has two sides: most update sentences that produced "no movement at all" under direct elicitation began to move, but a substantial share of them moved in the reverse direction, so the pooled agreement rate neither declined nor rose by much. The picture for the configurations accessed through agent harnesses is different: for codex the endpoint rate fell from 25% to 14% and the directional movement decreased by only about 2.5 points; for the four extension configurations the directional movement changed by between −3.7 and +0.5 points. In these five configurations the item-level pattern of the local models recurs on a smaller scale (zero movement nearly disappears, reversals rise, and the pooled agreement rate does not fall), but the point estimates of the net cost in directional sensitivity are all below the 5-point SESOI (the intervals do not exclude larger costs in every case; for gemini-3.8-flash (high) the 95% interval reaches −5.1). For the two gemini-3.8-flash configurations, which differ only in reasoning effort, the descriptive differences (high minus low) were +1.7 points for H1, 0.0 for H2, −0.073 for H4a and −1.3 points for H4b; no equivalence test was made.

This raises a question our data cannot yet answer: **does multi-estimate elicitation make the models' judgments more "correct," or does it merely make them respond somewhat less to everything?** In δ-NLI, the update **should** push the estimate in a given direction, but there is no reference for an appropriate magnitude, so a smaller average directional movement cannot yet be judged as an improvement or a decline in judgment quality. In other settings, information **should not** move the estimate (e.g., an irrelevant numerical anchor, or a known outcome when estimating the ex ante probability); if multi-estimate elicitation also reduced responses there, it would look like debiasing. Only by placing the two kinds of settings side by side can one tell whether multi-estimate elicitation leads models to "move when they should and stay put when they should not," or whether it simply dampens responses across the board. We list this test as future work (5.9).

The exploratory candidate-reading analysis (Tables 4 and 9) also shows why **MCW (Multi-Candidate Weighted)** and **MCU (Multi-Candidate Unweighted)** behave differently at the readout level (M_W vs. M_U). Across **14 of the 15 formal configurations** (6 of 7 in Table 4 and all 8 of 8 in Table 9), point estimates of directional movement strictly obey Top-1 > MCW (M_W) > MCU (M_U). For instance, in CLI-harnessed gpt-oss-120b (prior SD 15.7, stated posterior SD 14.0), ignoring the weights (MCU unweighted mean M_U) costs −5.9 points of directional movement, applying the model's weights (MCW M_W) cuts the loss to −2.3 points (within the 5-point SESOI), and taking Top-1 yields +1.7 points above Direct elicitation; similarly, in un-harnessed REST API aistudio:gemma-4-31b-it (prior SD 12.54, stated posterior SD 10.87), MCU (M_U) loses −6.98 points (exceeding the 5-point SESOI), whereas MCW (M_W) restricts the loss to −2.85 points and Top-1 reaches +0.64 points. In olmo2, by contrast—where weight-sum compliance within [0.95, 1.05] is only 22%–29%—MCU (−13.3), MCW (−13.7), and Top-1 (−14.5) are all equally degraded, and Top-1 moves by only about 5.5 points on average. One exploratory interpretation is that when a model assigns coherent weights under MCW, weighting allows the response to include lower-weight tail candidates without letting them dilute the focal directional update as heavily as equal 20% weighting under MCU (M_U); when candidates and weights are generated without coherent weighting, neither M_W nor Top-1 recovers the lost movement. Because these readings were conceived post hoc, and because the configurations are mutually confounded in model capability, vendor, training method, and calling method, we do not use them to make causal claims about "weaker models" in general.

The item-level reversal decomposition (Section 4.8) complements this readout gradient by highlighting a descriptive contrast between **expectation compliance (stereotypical homogenization)** and **counterfactual diversity**. The four CLI-harnessed frontier configurations (claude-sonnet-5.5, codex, gemini-3.8-flash low/high) achieve 95.8%–96.7% pair-level directional agreement with crowd annotations under Stated MCW Top-1, closely matching human annotators’ modal cultural scripts. Yet this alignment is accompanied by tight within-call clustering: even when prompted for five estimates under MCW, claude-sonnet-5.5 exhibits a stated posterior candidate SD of only 6.25 points (rounded to 6.3 in Table 4; codex 7.61 and gemini-3.8-flash (high) 7.53, rounded to 7.6 and 7.5 in Table 4), a mode-preserving tail counterfactual rate (TailCF%) of 5.8% (14/240 updates; 13.3% in codex and gemini-3.8-flash (high), and 15.8% in gemini-3.8-flash (low)), and almost unchanged extreme posterior mass (≤10% or ≥90% in 41.9% of Stated MCW M_W posteriors vs. 42.5% in Direct). Smaller and lightweight open-weight configurations (such as 4B-active MoE aistudio:gemma-4-26b-a4b-it and 4B–9B dense and hybrid sf:qwen3-8b, sf:glm-4-9b, and sf:qwen3.5-4b) more frequently surface non-stereotypical physical, temporal, or pragmatic interpretations (e.g., backyard rocket launches on `2275372714.jpg#3r1n`, ongoing stage performance vs. pre-concert practice on `5234657740.jpg#2r1n`, or rejecting implausible college-football age hearsay on `6338544926.jpg#0r1n`)—a descriptive pattern compatible with, though not causally isolating, weaker post-training typicality compression across model families. Under single-estimate Direct elicitation, committing to an atypical interpretation can produce a severe single-point reversal (as on `3275201035.jpg#2r1n`, where sf:qwen3.5-4b drops from 100% to 0% under Direct over a literal "black and red" bottle color mismatch, but recovers a +55.0-point Top-1 and +41.4-point M_W update under MCW by assigning 75% at weight 0.75 to Coca-Cola’s black-and-red label branding and 15%–2% to alternative brands); under unweighted MCU (M_U), equal 20% weighting allows lower-probability tail scenarios to reverse the aggregate update direction (as on `4541692312.jpg#4r1e`, where aistudio:gemma-4-26b-a4b-it matches claude-sonnet-5.5 and codex at Top-1 = 15% for an Apple Store demo unit while surfacing Genius Bar repair of a personal laptop at 35% and 55% with weights 0.30 and 0.15, yielding −60.0 points on Top-1 and −17.1 points on MCW M_W versus a +17.0-point reversal on unweighted MCU M_U; and in the OpenAI V3 bare-JSON arm, where MCW Top-1 reduces pair reversals to 3.3% against 23.3%–36.7% under unweighted MCU M_U). Paired with MCW, a 4B-active or 8B–9B open-weight model (aistudio:gemma-4-26b-a4b-it, sf:qwen3-8b, sf:glm-4-9b) can thus preserve the consensus direction in Top-1 and M_W while retaining 2.2×–2.9× wider stated candidate spread (SD 13.97–18.36 vs. 6.25) and 4.9×–6.2× higher TailCF% (28.6%–35.8% vs. 5.8%) than claude-sonnet-5.5.

The within-call spread of the five MCW candidates (S_multi) tells a complementary story (Section 4.7, exploratory). In all five CLI-harnessed configurations, four of eight un-harnessed REST API formal configurations, and high-capacity dev configurations, S_multi narrowed after a stated factual update and stayed wider after an anonymous report than after the same statement, which is what one would expect if weighted candidate spread reflected epistemic and source uncertainty; in the two small local models it did not. So the five estimates are not a uniform readout of uncertainty across all models, and studies that use verbalized candidate spread as an uncertainty measure should check this per model.

The REST API extension narrows the harness interpretation. Formal aistudio:gemma-4-31b-it has H4b = −2.85 (below the 5-point SESOI), Top-1 = +0.64, and spread −13.3% / +7.6%. Dev groq:gpt-oss-120b gives H4b = −4.15, Top-1 = +0.40 and spread −18.4% / +12.2%, compared descriptively with CLI-harnessed gpt-oss-120b (H4b = −2.30, spread −11% / +7%). Dev nv:nemotron-3-ultra-550b has H4b = −4.18, and ms:qwen3.5-27b has −2.07 with Top-1 +0.36. Small MCW directional costs and both spread signs therefore occur without CLI wrappers. Providers, model families and training pipelines remain observational confounds; these comparisons do not estimate a causal harness effect.

The controlled 1+1+4 diagnostic in Section 4.9 (Table 11) supplies more direct evidence comparing Direct (D0), Direct-CoT (D_CoT), prompt-level MCU (V2), and MCW (V0). First, Direct-CoT shows that explicit step-by-step reasoning before a single probability improves ordinal polarity discrimination (ROC-AUC 0.9189 / 0.9201), while non-copy first-numeric-token entropy H_num,nc^(1) remains below 1 bit (0.903 / 0.683 bits, near D0 at 0.765 / 0.649 bits), proposition binary entropy gain is small (ΔH2 +0.064 / +0.060 bits; endpoint mass 0.087 / 0.107 vs. 0.133 / 0.180 in D0), and Mean Δp vs. D0 is −2.37 / −1.83 points. Second, removing per-line weights in prompt-level MCU (V2) recovers H1_strength to +37.49 / +36.52 on gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4.1-mini and reduces unprompted draft-then-copy behavior on gpt-4.1-mini from 76.0% in MCW V0 to 26.0% in MCU V2 (consistent with an exploratory hypothesis of reduced simultaneous weight-assignment burden, although that mediating mechanism is not directly identified), whereas dropping only the sum-to-1 constraint (V1) does not restore movement and bare JSON (V3) suppresses pre-answer prose with a large movement loss on gpt-4o-mini. At the same time, omitting weights in MCU (V2) compresses within-call candidate spread by more than half (6.59 / 7.85 vs. 15.23 / 22.26 points in MCW V0) and cuts proposition binary entropy gain ΔH2 by more than half (+0.097 / +0.101 vs. +0.211 / +0.239 bits). Non-copy numeric-token entropy H_num,nc^(1) rises above 1 bit in both MCU (V2, 1.219 / 1.079 bits) and MCW (V0 on gpt-4o-mini at 1.261 bits; V3 at 1.400 / 1.325 bits), but is conditional on generated prefixes and should not be equated with calibrated epistemic uncertainty. The ablation is restricted to two models and 30 dev pairs.

The original Verbalized Sampling paper discusses gains in diversity and reports that stronger models benefit more (Zhang et al., 2025); the present study measures directional sensitivity, and the two are different dependent variables. Whether the effect of multi-estimate elicitation on directional sensitivity varies with model capability needs to be tested directly, after controlling for vendor and calling method.

### 5.5 Implications for multi-candidate elicitation and long-context reasoning

Multi-candidate formats such as Verbalized Sampling are attractive because they counter mode collapse—reducing extreme answers and exposing a distribution rather than a single number. Beyond single-step judgment tasks, this trade-off matters for how LLMs process long contexts in retrieval-augmented generation, multi-document synthesis, and agentic workflows. Relative to any single belief or hypothesis, a long context is effectively a sequence of sentences that strengthen, weaken, or report claims about that belief. Under direct elicitation, a model that suffers from endpoint crowding (collapsing onto ≤5% or ≥95%) or round-number inertia can lock into an extreme belief early in the context, leaving subsequent strengthening or weakening sentences with no room to register. Conversely, our results show that forcing multiple candidates to break mode collapse has sharply different side effects across models: in the two small local models, the extra candidates act like uncalibrated dispersion that cuts sensitivity to valid directional evidence by roughly half and did not exhibit the expected spread pattern, whereas in the five harnessed configurations the point estimates of the cost to directional sensitivity are small (below 4 points, though the interval for gemini-3.8-flash (high) reaches −5.1 points) and the candidate spread narrows with stated facts while remaining wider under hearsay.

A lower endpoint rate, on its own, therefore does not show that an elicitation format has improved a model's belief updating. The present protocol provides a lightweight, black-box behavioral diagnostic that can be run before adopting such a format for a given model. It requires only items whose evidence has a known direction, two separate calls per item, and the two elicitation formats; its outputs—endpoint reduction (H4a), directional sensitivity cost (H4b), the candidate-reading gradient (unweighted mean vs. weighted mean vs. top-weighted candidate; Table 4), and candidate-spread dynamics (Table 8)—form a response profile that distinguishes models whose candidate distributions track evidence from those whose candidates act mainly as noisy dispersion. We use "profile" descriptively: even with 15 formal and 20 dev configurations, model scale, vendor, training and calling method remain confounded, and whether these profiles generalize to other judgment tasks is the subject of the follow-up study mentioned in 5.9.

For smaller models whose standard weighted MCW format (V0) exhibits wide uncalibrated dispersion (SD > 15 points) and reduced directional movement under M_W, practical options within the MCV family are to inspect Top-1 alongside M_W, elicit unweighted candidates via MCU (V2, trading candidate spread for preserved directional movement), or run this four-metric diagnostic (H4a, H4b, the Top-1 > MCW > MCU gradient, and S_multi spread dynamics) before selecting an elicitation format. Table 11 supports these options for the two tested OpenAI models; designing structured second-generation MCW scaffolds that preserve directional movement on smaller models without sacrificing candidate spread on tasks with objective ground truth remains an important direction for follow-up work (Section 5.9). More broadly, when downstream applications require surfacing plausible counterfactual exceptions alongside the mainstream expectation—such as risk auditing or multi-hypothesis tracking—pairing a lightweight open-weight model (e.g., 4B-active aistudio:gemma-4-26b-a4b-it or 8B sf:qwen3-8b) with MCW and reading out (Top-1, M_W, S_multi) jointly can provide richer counterfactual coverage (TailCF% 31.2%–35.8%) than a narrow-spread frontier model whose candidates cluster tightly around the single most stereotypical script (TailCF% 5.8%–15.8%), without suffering the directional dilution of unweighted MCU.

### 5.6 Limitations

- **Materials**: δ-SNLI was released in 2020 and may already be present in training corpora; the effect of contamination on each condition is unknown. The direction of an update reflects the writer's intent; in the original paper, about 8.9% of weakeners were judged by validators to be strengtheners. Our direction spot check involved only one human rater (the author), covered 20 items, and examined only the stated version. The premises come from image captions and tend to be short.
- **Wording**: Reporting used only one wording ("Someone said that"), and the lead-in sentence of the stated version itself signals that the information is true; H2 compares the total effect of these two wordings, and its conclusions are limited to these two wordings.
- **Measurement**: The prior and the posterior come from two independent calls; Δ is "the difference between the probabilities reported under two inputs," not a revision within a single reasoning process. Single reported values from the local models are noisy. Parsing rule v2 was revised after seeing the format failures on the development set (the probability values were not examined during the revision).
- **Models**: Fifteen formal configurations include five accessed through CLI harnesses, whose system prompts, sampling settings and server-side updates remain outside our control. The eight un-harnessed dnli-api formal and 20 dev configurations, including Qwen3-8B and gpt-oss-120b comparisons across calling methods, reduce the scope of a harness-only explanation; they do not remove differences across providers, families and post-training recipes. The API and CLI extensions use only the 120 Block-A pairs. The cross-scale ladder uses 30 dev pairs (300 planned units per configuration); the 1+1+4 ablation uses those same 30 pairs (900 calls per model). These development diagnostics are exploratory, and their generality and precision are limited, especially where missingness is substantial. Temperature and sampling settings were not systematically varied.

- **Reference**: Without human probabilities under the same paradigm, we cannot judge whether movement is excessive or insufficient.

Section 3.1 describes the pre-specified exclusion of items involving demographic or sensitive attributes, including the scope and limits of model-based screening. ### 5.9 Future work

1. **Human reference**: Ask people to report priors and posteriors on the same set of pairs (multiple raters per item), to answer the magnitude question and to test whether the reporting wording also "shifts the direction of movement" for humans.
2. **Reasoning effort and capability**: The extension compared two reasoning efforts of one model only descriptively. A design that varies effort within several models, and capability within one vendor, is needed to say whether the cost of multi-estimate elicitation depends on either.
3. **"Should move" versus "should not move"**: Carry multi-estimate elicitation over to classic paradigms in which information should not move the estimate, such as anchoring (an irrelevant number) and hindsight bias (estimating the ex ante probability when the outcome is known), as well as base-rate problems, in which information should be used but is often neglected. Our preliminary literature search did not find studies that apply the "verbalize multiple candidates at once" elicitation to these paradigms. A follow-up study along these lines is in preparation.
4. **Variants of reporting**: Named sources, sources of differing credibility, and direct versus indirect quotation, to separate the two explanations of pragmatic implication and source credibility.
5. **Verbalized distributions versus token-level log-probabilities**: The original Verbalized Sampling study used base-model sequence log-probabilities to diagnose typicality bias in preference data and tested perplexity and negative log-likelihood as verbalized formats (Zhang et al., 2025). On open-weight models where full next-token distributions are accessible, comparing the verbalized candidate spread and weights against token-level log-probability entropy at the emitted probability tokens can directly test how closely verbalized uncertainty tracks internal logit uncertainty across model scales. Our 30-pair 1+1+4 logprobs ablation provides an initial token-entropy comparison on two closed API models (gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4.1-mini). Extending this comparison to full formal splits and open-weight models across scales remains future work.
