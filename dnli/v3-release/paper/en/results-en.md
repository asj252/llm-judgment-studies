## 4 Results

Sections 4.1–4.5 report the three original configurations (Qwen3, OLMo-2, codex); Section 4.6 reports the four CLI-harnessed extension configurations run with the same protocol; Section 4.7 examines candidate spread across all 15 formal configurations; Section 4.8 reports the eight un-harnessed REST API formal configurations and the 20-configuration development ladder (dnli-api); Section 4.9 presents the controlled six-arm format, weight-constraint, CoT, and token-level log-probability ablation; Section 4.10 summarizes the main findings.

### 4.1 Sample and completeness

All planned units of the three original configurations were fully reconciled (every planned unit accounted for) (Table 1). qwen3-off and codex had no missing units; olmo2 was missing 3 units (0.12%), all under multi-estimate elicitation, and following the pre-specified rule we computed an additional conservative interval with worst-case imputation of missing units. No attempt was truncated. The formal run of codex was rerun under a new experiment fingerprint (a hash binding the plan, sampling, prompts, parser, runner and materials) after Addendum A1 (calls that fail to connect to the server do not invalidate the session); the earlier results do not enter the analysis.

### 4.2 Directional sensitivity (H1)

Under stated presentation and direct elicitation, all three configurations moved in the direction annotated for the update, and for all three the Holm-adjusted bound exceeds the smallest effect size of interest (SESOI) of 5 points (Table 2; Figure 1): the directional movement *s* is 18.6, 20.0 and 25.3 points. On average, strengtheners raised the probability by 15.9, 14.4 and 25.9 points, and weakeners lowered it by 21.3, 25.6 and 24.7 points; the difference D between the posteriors after strengtheners and after weakeners is 37.2, 40.1 and 50.6 points, respectively.

Item by item, the proportion of moves whose direction agrees with the annotation is 0.66, 0.55 and 0.83, and the proportion of reverse moves is 0.15, 0.16 and 0.09; the remainder report the same number before and after (qwen3-off: 23% for strengtheners, 16% for weakeners; olmo2: 29% for both; codex: 9% and 8%). That is, counted by update condition, 16%–29% of update sentences left the reported probability of the local models unchanged.

### 4.3 Reporting wording (H2)

When the same update was rephrased as "Someone said that …", the directional movement became smaller in all three configurations: stated minus reported is 2.0, 4.8 and 5.4 points (Table 2). All three pass the directional test under Holm correction. For codex the point estimate reaches the 5-point SESOI, but the Holm-adjusted bound (3.85) does not exceed it; for the two local configurations the point estimate is below the SESOI.

The **reversal rate** under reported presentation is higher than under stated presentation in all three configurations: qwen3-off 0.146 → 0.173, olmo2 0.163 → 0.221, codex 0.092 → 0.117. Following the pre-specified interpretation rule, we state the conclusion as **reporting shifts the direction of movement**: after the update is recast as a report, movement in the annotated direction is smaller, and at the same time more items move in the opposite direction. This is not the same as "discounting within the same direction."

Broken down by update type (descriptive; no per-type tests were run, and there is no evidence of equivalence), the three configurations show different patterns (Table 3): for olmo2 the difference lies almost entirely in strengtheners (stated +14.4, reported +4.7), while the means for weakeners are close (−25.6 vs. −25.7; equivalence not tested); for codex the difference lies mainly in weakeners (−24.7, −16.5); for qwen3-off both types decrease only slightly. This is a post hoc observation, offered only as exploratory description.

### 4.4 Multi-estimate elicitation (H4a, H4b)

**Endpoints (H4a).** Under direct elicitation, a large share of responses fall at ≤5% or ≥95% (Figure 2 shows the unweighted rate in each condition): the endpoint rate, computed with the pre-specified weights (prior 1/2, stated strengthener 1/4, stated weakener 1/4), is 29.6%, 42.0% and 25.2%. With multi-estimate elicitation (listing 5 estimates, each with a weight, and taking the weighted mean), it drops to 4.3%, 4.9% and 14.4%. The differences 0.25, 0.37 and 0.11 all pass Holm correction; for the two local configurations the adjusted bound exceeds the SESOI of 10 percentage points, while for codex the point estimate reaches the SESOI but the adjusted bound does not (Table 2).

**Directional sensitivity (H4b).** Multi-estimate elicitation reduces the directional movement: multi-estimate minus direct is −7.9, −13.7 and −2.5 points. For the two local configurations the adjusted bound lies beyond −5 points (the whole interval falls below −5 points); for codex the direction is confirmed and the point estimate is below the SESOI (Table 2).

Taken together, multi-estimate elicitation reduced endpoint values in all three configurations; in the two local models it also reduced the mean directional movement, by about 43% (qwen3-off 18.6 → 10.7) and 68% (olmo2 20.0 → 6.3, 238 pairs), respectively; for codex the directional movement decreased by about 2.5 points (25.3 → 22.8), with the direction confirmed and the point estimate below the 5-point SESOI.

Viewed by update condition, the change in the local models is not merely a smaller magnitude: under multi-estimate elicitation, **the proportion of zero movement falls and the proportion of reverse movement rises**. Zero movement: qwen3-off strengtheners 22.9% → 4.6%, weakeners 16.3% → 5.8%; olmo2 29.2% → 0.4%, 29.2% → 1.3%. Reverse movement: qwen3-off 0.14 → 0.34, 0.15 → 0.24; olmo2 0.17 → 0.41, 0.16 → 0.40. The pooled agreement rate, however, did not fall (qwen3-off 0.658 → 0.658, olmo2 0.546 → 0.586): update sentences that originally produced no movement moved, under multi-estimate elicitation, some in the annotated direction and some in the opposite direction. For codex the reversal rate is similar to that under direct elicitation (0.067 → 0.067, 0.117 → 0.125), and the pooled agreement rate goes from 0.825 to 0.871.

**Candidate level (descriptive).** Under the stated condition, the weighted standard deviation of the 5 estimates is 16.1 points for qwen3-off, 18.3 points for olmo2 and 7.6 points for codex. The proportion of responses whose weights sum to a value in [0.95, 1.05] is 93–94% for qwen3-off, 22–29% for olmo2 and 100% for codex (non-conforming weights are normalized by their sum; the weighted mean is invariant to overall scaling).

**Readings within the candidates: internal coherence of MCW versus MCU (exploratory, not pre-specified).** To distinguish "averaging pulls dispersed candidates toward the middle" from "the candidates or their weights fail to track the evidence", we recomputed the directional movement post hoc with three additional readings alongside the pre-specified **MCW** weighted mean (M_W): the **MCU** unweighted arithmetic mean of the 5 candidates (M_U), the top-weighted modal candidate (Top-1), and the weighted median (Table 4, which also includes the four Section 4.6 extension configurations across all four readings; Figure 3 plots the three primary configurations and the three weighted readings: weighted mean, top-weighted candidate, and lower weighted median; maximum-weight ties in Top-1 are averaged). For qwen3-off, ignoring weights (MCU unweighted mean M_U) worsens the difference from direct elicitation from −7.9 to −10.3 points, whereas taking the top-weighted candidate shrinks it to −4.6 points (the directional movement of the top-weighted candidate remains about 14.0 points); for olmo2, the unweighted mean (−13.3), weighted mean (−13.7), and top-weighted candidate (−14.5) are all similarly degraded, and the top-weighted candidate moves by only about 5.5 points; for codex, all four readings lie between −3.1 and −2.1 points. Across the extension configurations (Section 4.6), the same internal coherence ordering (Top-1 > MCW (M_W) > MCU (M_U), i.e., top-weighted greater than weighted mean, which is greater than unweighted mean) holds in every configuration, most visibly in gpt-oss-120b, where candidates are widely dispersed (stated posterior SD 14.0): the unweighted mean (MCU, M_U) loses −5.9 points relative to direct elicitation, the weighted mean (MCW, M_W) cuts the loss to −2.3 points, and the top-weighted candidate (Top-1) gains +1.7 points. Combined with the eight un-harnessed REST API formal configurations in Section 4.8 (Table 9), **14 of the 15 formal configurations** (all except OLMo-2, whose weight-sum compliance within [0.95, 1.05] is only 22%–29%) strictly obey Top-1 > MCW (M_W) > MCU (M_U) in point estimate. This describes the consistency of candidate-level readings across models and does not by itself identify a causal mechanism: model capability, vendor, training method and CLI harness are confounded with one another, and the primary configurations alone cannot separate "candidates interfere with one another" from "format burden". These analyses were conceived only after we had seen the formal results and are offered only as exploration.

### 4.5 Robustness and supplementary description

- **Quoted-speech updates** (updates whose reported version becomes quoted speech embedded in a report; pre-specified; of the 11 IDs on the frozen list, the formal set contains 7 sentences, involving 7 pairs): after excluding these 7 pairs and recomputing, the H1, H2 and H4b conclusions are unchanged in all three configurations (e.g., H2: 2.05, 4.92, 5.48 points; codex was run only on Block A, leaving 117 pairs after exclusion).
- **The common 120 pairs**: on the same 120 pairs used for codex, the two local configurations' point estimates for H1, H2, H4a and H4b have the same direction as on the 240 pairs, although the values differ (e.g., olmo2 H1 22.0 vs. 20.0, H2 6.2 vs. 4.8; for qwen3-off, H2 is 1.7 points, with interval [−0.6, 3.9] spanning 0). Cross-configuration comparisons are descriptive only.
- **Stratification by material source** (the dev and test splits of δ-SNLI, unrelated to this study's development set): the direction is consistent across the two strata; H2 is larger on items from dev in all three configurations.
- **Between-call variability** (repeat subset of 20 pairs): when the same prompt was asked a second time, the mean absolute difference between the two reported values was 9.6, 7.7 and 2.3 points under direct elicitation, and 7.7, 9.5 and 2.0 points under multi-estimate elicitation. Under stated presentation, the mean absolute difference in *s* is 5.7, 7.2 and 2.3 points, whereas the between-item standard deviation is 16.9, 23.7 and 15.3 points. Single reported values from the local models are very noisy; the conclusions rest on averages over 240 pairs and are not suited to interpreting individual items.
- **Comparison with the development set**: on the development set (30 pairs), H2 was 4.3, 4.6 and 5.7 points (7.0 for codex on the development set rerun after Addendum A1); on the formal set, qwen3-off is about half its development-set value, and the others are close. The H4b pattern of "large differences for the local models, a small difference for codex" recurs in the formal set.

### 4.6 Extension: four further configurations (v2)

The four extension configurations (Section 3.3) were run on the same 120 Block-A pairs and the same 20-pair repeat subset as codex, with the same prompts, parser (with one input normalization; Section 3.3), hypotheses, SESOIs and Holm families; each configuration forms its own family {H1, H2, H4a, H4b}. All planned units were reconciled and none were missing (Table 6). Retries were rare: in the final records, 5, 5, 8 and 5 earlier attempts were discarded, almost all because the event stream contained a step other than the answer (classified as possible tool use and resampled), and three because of a timeout; counted by the run's persistent retry counter, which also covers sessions that were later resumed, gemini-3.8-flash (low) had 10 retries and the others 5. According to the run log, six units of gemini-3.8-flash (low) were blocked at least once by the provider's content filter, which surfaced as such a step; one of them was blocked three times in a row and stopped the run. On review (recorded in the run's review log), the stop was cleared once so that the unit could be retried, because blocks appeared to be stochastic (other conditions of the same pair had passed on retry); we decided at the same time that units blocked persistently would be recorded as missing and reported in an addendum. The retried unit was answered on its next attempts, so this provision was never needed. All six units therefore have valid answers, and no unit is missing for this reason. Service errors that occurred before a conversation started also stopped the run and were cleared under a written rule before resuming (Section 3.6); such calls produced no answer and leave no attempt in the analysed record.

These contrasts are not part of any Holm family.

### 4.7 Candidate spread (exploratory, not pre-specified)

Under multi-estimate elicitation each response gives five candidate probabilities with weights, so it also carries a spread: the weighted standard deviation of the candidates (weights normalized to sum to 1; SD = √Σ wᵢ(xᵢ − m)², where m is the weighted mean). We asked three questions after seeing the formal results: does the spread shrink after an update, is it larger after a report than after a stated update, and do items with a more dispersed prior move more? Intervals are cluster-bootstrap 95% intervals (B = 2,000, seed 20261500); none of these analyses enters a Holm family. Effective complete-pair counts are n = 240 for Qwen3 (thinking off), n = 236–238 for OLMo-2, n = 120 Block-A pairs each for codex and the four dnli-agy configurations, and n = 115–120 for the eight dnli-api configurations (Table 9). Spearman ρ assigns average ranks to ties and is undefined if either variable has zero variance across pairs; its bootstrap interval is undefined if any resample is degenerate (0 undefined resamples occurred across all seven configurations).

The two local models show no consistent pattern: in Qwen3 a stated strengthener widened the spread (+2.6 points) and a weakener narrowed it (−2.1), so the average barely moves, and after a report the spread was smaller, not larger; in OLMo-2 the changes were near zero. Four candidate factors emerge as exploratory, post-hoc empirical associations across the 15 formal and 20 dev configurations:

1. **Total and active scale within families.** On Groq, gpt-oss-120b gives −18.4% / +12.2%, versus +4.3% / +3.3% for gpt-oss-20b. Formal Gemma 31B dense shows twice the stated narrowing of 4B-active Gemma MoE. Large total size is insufficient: 29B dense sf:xing4.0-29b gives −4.3% / −4.2%.
2. **MoE routing capacity.** Nemotron 30B-A3B gives +8.0% / −9.3%, whereas 120B-A12B and 550B-A55B show both expected signs. These contrasts vary absolute active scale as well as active-to-total ratio and do not isolate routing causally.
3. **Family and instruction-tuning generation.** On SiliconFlow, Qwen2.5 7B widens on both contrasts (+4.7% / +3.5%); Qwen3 8B narrows on both (−2.4% / −6.2%), matching local Qwen3’s reported contrast (−13%); GLM 9B shows both expected signs. Similar size does not imply similar spread behavior.

These associations have exceptions, and provider, family, post-training and calling settings remain confounded. They motivate controlled tests rather than a causal ranking of factors.

**Prompt-level MCU (V2) versus MCW (V0).** Removing the per-line Weight field (MCU, V2) raises H1_strength from +26.49 / +23.91 in standard weighted MCW (V0) to +37.49 / +36.52, and overall ROC-AUC from 0.8549 / 0.8631 to 0.9074 / 0.9115, matching or exceeding Direct D0 (0.9064 / 0.8633). However, omitting weights also compresses mean within-call candidate SD by more than half—from 15.23 / 22.26 points in MCW (V0) to 6.59 / 7.85 points in MCU (V2)—and cuts the proposition binary entropy gain ΔH2 by more than half (+0.097 / +0.101 bits in MCU V2 versus +0.211 / +0.239 bits in MCW V0; endpoint mass 0.085 / 0.109 in MCU V2 versus 0.063 / 0.184 in MCW V0, and mean signed probability difference vs. D0 of −5.23 / −5.05 versus −8.67 / −8.79 points). Thus, while prompt-level MCU (V2) recovers H1_strength on these two smaller models (consistent with an exploratory hypothesis of reduced simultaneous weight-assignment burden, though that cognitive mechanism is not directly observed), it does so alongside clustering the five candidates much closer to a single mode rather than spanning a wide weighted distribution; conversely, within MCW (V0), reading out Top-1 (mean signed difference vs. D0 of +3.23 / −3.35 points) avoids the larger downward shift of unweighted M_U (−17.62 / −10.77 points).

**Weight constraint (V1) and bare JSON (V3).** Removing the sum-to-1 instruction (MCW-freew, V1) lowers weight-sum compliance within [0.95, 1.05] from 100.0% to 89.3% / 81.3%, while H1_strength remains low (+24.48 / +26.45). Bare JSON without prose (MCW-json, V3) reduces mean completion length to 61.0 / 80.0 tokens, versus 175.1 / 368.9 in V0; on gpt-4o-mini it further lowers H1_strength to +13.40 (ROC-AUC 0.7928). Weight-sum compliance nevertheless remains 99.3% / 100.0%. Formatting compliance alone therefore does not ensure directional sensitivity.

**Explicit single-estimate reasoning (Direct-CoT).** Direct-CoT (D_CoT) asks for 2–4 sentences before a single estimate and gives the highest overall ROC-AUC (0.9189 / 0.9201). On gpt-4.1-mini it narrows the stated-minus-reported AUC gap from +0.0900 (0.9072 vs. 0.8172) to +0.0372 (0.9394 vs. 0.9022). However, Direct-CoT yields only a small proposition binary entropy gain relative to D0 (ΔH2 of +0.064 / +0.060 bits; endpoint mass 0.087 / 0.107 vs. 0.133 / 0.180 in D0; Mean Δp vs. D0 of −2.37 / −1.83 points across the 150 matched contexts) and leaves non-copy first-numeric-token entropy below 1 bit (0.903 / 0.683 bits). AUC measures ordinal polarity discrimination independently of probability scale; step-by-step reasoning before a single number sharpens polarity discrimination without opening a multi-candidate distribution.

**Numeric-token entropy and copying across MCV arms.** In gpt-4.1-mini, the draft-then-copy heuristic flags 76.0% (114/150; n_noncopy_units = 25) of MCW V0 and 75.3% (113/150; n_noncopy_units = 27) of V1 calls, versus 26.0% (39/150; n_noncopy_units = 110) in unweighted MCU V2 and 0.0% (0/150; n_noncopy_units = 150) in bare JSON V3. In gpt-4o-mini, it flags 8.7% (13/150; n_noncopy_units = 137) in V0 and 0.0% (150/150 non-copy) in V1–V3. This is consistent with gpt-4.1-mini using an unprompted draft-then-copy strategy in prose to coordinate simultaneous candidate generation and weight assignment under MCW. Raw first-numeric-token entropy in gpt-4.1-mini V0/V1 is consequently near zero (0.120 / 0.172 bits), consistent with copying previously drafted numbers. The ≥0.01-bit non-copy restriction is an operational filter rather than a direct observation of an internal process. Under that filter, single-estimate Direct D0 entropy of 0.765 / 0.649 bits (and Direct-CoT 0.903 / 0.683 bits) rises to 1.219 / 1.079 bits in MCU V2 and 1.261–1.454 / 0.720–1.325 bits across the MCW arms (V0/V1/V3), alongside positive proposition binary entropy shifts (+0.097 to +0.303 bits). Token entropy is conditional on the generated prefix and top-20 support; it is distinct from both proposition binary entropy and the dispersion of verbalized candidates.

### 4.10 Summary

1. All 15 formal configurations move in the annotated direction (H1: +16.1 to +28.9 points) and reject H1. The three formal tiers comprise 23,400 planned units.
2. Reporting reduces movement significantly in 13 of 15 formal configurations (+2.0 to +8.9 points); the exceptions are gpt-oss-120b (+0.9) and sf:qwen3.5-4b (−0.64). H4a rejects in all 15 (+0.065 to +0.391 endpoint-rate proportion).
3. Within the Multi-Candidate Verbalization (MCV) family, MCW movement costs (H4b) vary widely (−13.7 to +0.5 points), while 14 of 15 formal configurations obey the exploratory point-estimate ordering Top-1 > MCW (M_W) > MCU (M_U). Small MCW costs and stated narrowing / reported widening also occur without a CLI harness. Item-level reversal decomposition shows that 44.9%–77.3% of Stated Direct update reversals are low-amplitude (|Δ| ≤ 10 points) and 53.8%–84.2% preserve pair ordering (s_i ≥ 0), while open-weight configurations under MCW retain 19.4%–36.7% mode-preserving tail counterfactuals (TailCF%, reaching 28.6%–36.7% in sf:glm-4-9b, Qwen3-8B, sf:qwen3-8b, aistudio:gemma-4-26b-a4b-it, and gpt-oss-120b) compared with 5.8%–15.8% in the four narrow-spread CLI-harnessed frontier configurations. The 20-model dev ladder gives exploratory cross-scale associations, while the two-model 1+1+4 ablation shows that Direct-CoT keeps token entropy below 1 bit with small proposition entropy gains, and that prompt-level MCU (V2) recovers directional movement on smaller models while halving within-call candidate spread and proposition entropy gain relative to MCW (V0).
